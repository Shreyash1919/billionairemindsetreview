Binaural beats benefits, reviewed

Updated September 2026 · Review page · contains affiliate links

Binaural beats are one of the most popular relaxation audio tools online. Here's what they are, what evidence suggests, and how to use them sensibly.

How binaural beats work

Play a slightly different tone in each ear, say 200 Hz on the left and 206 Hz on the right, and your brain perceives a pulsing "beat" at the difference between them, in this case 6 Hz. That perceived pulse is the binaural beat. Because the effect happens inside your auditory processing, headphones are essential; through speakers it doesn't work the same way.

What the research suggests

Reviews of brainwave entrainment have found that audio frequencies can influence brainwave activity and certain psychological states under controlled conditions. Studies have looked at anxiety reduction, relaxation, attention and sleep quality. Results are mixed and often modest: some trials show benefits, others find little difference from ordinary calming music, and effects tend to depend on the person and how consistently they use it.

The honest summary: promising for relaxation and possibly focus, not a proven performance drug, and not a treatment for any medical condition.

How to use them properly

  1. Use stereo headphones. Without them, the effect doesn't happen.
  2. Keep the volume low. It should be comfortable background sound, not loud.
  3. Be consistent. Same time daily, usually morning or just before bed, for a few weeks.
  4. Do one thing. Sit still or work quietly; don't drive or operate machinery while listening.
  5. Track how you feel in a note on your phone, rather than relying on memory.

Safety notes

Audio programs are not medical treatment. If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, talk to your doctor before using rhythmic audio or visual entrainment. If you have ongoing anxiety, low mood or sleep problems, see a healthcare professional rather than relying on an audio track. Never listen while driving.

Free versus paid

There are free binaural and ambient tracks on streaming platforms, and there are paid programs built around a specific routine and framing. Free is a sensible way to discover whether you even like the sound. Paid programs mainly add structure, packaging and, sometimes, a guarantee. If you don't enjoy free tracks, a paid one is unlikely to change that.
